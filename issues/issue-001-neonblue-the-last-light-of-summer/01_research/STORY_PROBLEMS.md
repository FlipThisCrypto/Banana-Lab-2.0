# Issue 001 — Story Problems

The writing is the strongest thing the legacy issue has. These are the problems
that remain, and they are fixable without redesigning the story.

---

## 1. The Echo fires on presence, not on choice

The season bible is unambiguous: the relay activates **only after** NeonBlue
chooses the unseen group over the spotlight. The legacy script has the control
box flare cyan on page 4, at the midpoint, before the crisis exists.

The cost is thematic. The issue's stated answer is *"NeonBlue's hope is not
ignorance. It becomes stronger when it makes room for risk."* If the system
lights up simply because he walks over, the story says he is special by
default — the opposite argument. It also spends the issue's biggest visual
moment two pages before its biggest emotional moment, leaving the climax with
nothing to look at.

**Fix** Page 4 gets an ambiguous flicker that could be coincidence. The real
activation lands on page 8, after the rescue. Tracked as conflict C-01.

---

## 2. Lil Devil has no story function

The bible gives the guest one job: his impatience *accidentally* reveals that
the failures are not random. In the legacy script he cracks his knuckles, grabs
a gate, and later hits a hinge where he is told to. The discovery happens
without him.

That leaves him as a tool rather than a character, and it strands his growth
seed — *"learns that restraint can direct power"* — because he never gets the
uncontrolled action that the restraint is a lesson about.

**Fix** He forces the service gate early, against advice. The system's response
to *that* interference is what exposes the pattern. Tracked as conflict C-02.

---

## 3. The climax collapses to one contributor

The bible's climax: NeonBlue *"directs Lil Devil's force, Static's warning, and
TwoTone's route analysis."* Three characters, one plan, one leader.

The legacy climax has Lil Devil strike a hinge. Static and TwoTone, having done
the investigative work in the first half, do nothing at the moment it pays off.

**Fix** Three beats on page 7: TwoTone names the route, Static calls the timing
window, Lil Devil applies force where NeonBlue marks. Same page count.

---

## 4. Scarline disappears

One line on page 2 ("Dark isn't the problem. What people do in it is."), then
nothing for six pages. The bible requires every supporting character to have a
purposeful function and gives Scarline a specific one: validation — helping
others recognise their own correct choice.

**Fix** She gets the beat at NeonBlue's decision point. Not "go rescue them" —
that would take the choice away, which is precisely what her character does not
do. Something closer to confirming that the choice in front of him is real.
Tracked as conflict C-06.

---

## 5. The dialogue is written for prose, not for balloons

Representative legacy lines:

> "Six monkeys, three main zones, eight rides! We're not just attending the
> festival, we're optimizing it!"

> "Power isn't bleeding out—it's being drawn away from the stage into the old
> maintenance conduits."

> "That wasn't a brownout. The frequency dropped an octave... like it's stepping
> down on purpose."

These run 18–25 words. At comic lettering sizes a balloon that long covers a
third of the panel, which is part of why the legacy pages have so little room
for staging. They also over-explain: the second line tells the reader what the
art should show.

**Fix** Target 12–15 words per balloon, maximum two balloons per panel, and let
the image carry what the image can carry.

---

## 6. Invented specifics that constrain later issues

| Legacy line | Problem |
|---|---|
| "a thirteen-year-old system" | Dates FusionZoo infrastructure. The bible reserves this; Issue 003 is meant to establish the system's age and Issue 006 keeps its origin open. |
| "Five thousand people" | Precise crowd figure with nothing to support it, and no crowd is drawn. |
| "three main zones, eight rides" | Invents festival geography that no location bible describes. |

**Fix** Remove all three. Tracked as conflict C-04.

---

## 7. NeonBlue's crack is told, not dramatised

The bible's midpoint: *"His cheerful language becomes more exaggerated as his
fear increases."* The legacy script gives him one panel of forced cheer
("Keep smiling, everybody! Smiles generate ambient morale! Science says so!")
and then a caption reading "Optimism as armor". The caption does the work the
performance should do.

**Fix** Escalate across three separated beats — brighter, faster, thinner — so
the reader feels it tighten before Moodz names it. Captions stop explaining
what the reader can already see.

---

## 8. The trapped group is anonymous

"A whole group trapped behind the emergency shutter" — unnamed, unseen, never
depicted. NeonBlue's choice is between a crowd he can see and an abstraction.
That is not a hard choice, and it is not a moving one.

**Fix** Three specific young festival-goers with distinct silhouettes,
established one page before the rescue. No new named characters and no new
canon. Tracked as conflict C-07.

---

## 9. The resolution lands, and should be protected

> "Main stage finale was half-lit. Carousel stayed off. But everyone walked out
> safe. That's a good night."

This is the best line in the legacy script. It does exactly what the bible asks:
NeonBlue does not sell the night as perfect, and he says in his own voice that
they got everyone out and that is enough.

Keep it. Trim it slightly for balloon length. Do not improve it.

---

## 10. Structural summary

| Problem | Severity | Fix cost |
|---|---|---|
| Echo fires before the choice | CRITICAL | Move one beat; adjust pages 4, 7, 8 |
| Lil Devil has no function | CRITICAL | Rewrite two beats |
| Climax uses one of three contributors | MAJOR | Redistribute page 7 |
| Scarline vanishes | MAJOR | Add one beat |
| Balloon-hostile dialogue | MAJOR | Line edit throughout |
| Invented canon specifics | MAJOR | Delete three phrases |
| Optimism crack is captioned | MINOR | Re-stage three beats |
| Anonymous trapped group | MAJOR | Establish them one page earlier |

None of these requires redesigning the story, the characters, or the season
arc. The legacy script is a sound draft that drifted from its bible in specific,
correctable ways.
