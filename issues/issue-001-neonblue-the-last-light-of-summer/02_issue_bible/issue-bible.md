## What the title means

"The Last Light of Summer" is a theme, a lighting plan and a plot description at
once, and the issue should use all three readings.

**Narratively** it is the final night of the festival season — the last shared
warm evening before things get harder. The season runs August to January and
gets colder, darker and more difficult; this is the issue where everyone is
still happy.

**Literally** it is about light going out. A travelling blackout eats the
festival section by section. The "last light" is the one that matters — the one
down a corridor where nobody is looking.

**Emotionally** it is NeonBlue. His optimism is the last light: the thing he
holds up so nobody has to sit in the dark. The issue asks what happens when
holding it up stops being honest.

### As a lighting instruction

The title drives a controlled progression across the eight pages. This is not
decoration; it is a production requirement recorded in the layout spec and the
background staging guides.

| Pages | Light | Palette | Feeling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low warm sun, long shadows, festival lights just coming on | Amber, gold, warm magenta | The good version of the evening |
| 2–3 | Sun gone. Artificial light only, and patchy | Cooling — magenta and cyan take over from amber | Something is wrong but manageable |
| 4–5 | Full night. Hard practical sources, deep shadow | Cyan-dominant, high contrast | Isolation, machinery, the past |
| 6 | Darkest page. The corridor has almost nothing | Near-monochrome with one red practical | The choice |
| 7 | Working light. Torches, sparks, restored partial power | Warm returning, still fighting cyan | Effort |
| 8 | Restored but reduced. Half the festival is lit | Warm and cyan coexisting, neither winning | Earned, imperfect |

The last panel puts a single cyan segment behind NeonBlue, unseen. Cyan has
belonged to the failure all issue. At the end it belongs to something else.

**Restraint required.** Do not put a sunset in every panel. Pages 4 through 7
are indoors or after dark; a literal sunset there would be wrong. The
progression lives in the *quality and colour of light*, not in visible sky.

## The story in one paragraph

NeonBlue volunteers the team for more than they agreed to, promises the backup
system will hold, and is immediately proved wrong. As the outages travel, the
team works out that the failure is patterned — and Lil Devil, forcing a locked
gate he was told to leave alone, provokes a reaction that shows the system is
responding to interference rather than simply breaking. In a service corridor,
NeonBlue finds a relay marker carrying a frequency Patch once used. His
reassurance turns brittle; Ash names it in five words and Moodz names it
kindly. When the final countdown pulls everyone to the main stage, three young
festival-goers are found trapped behind a jammed shutter. NeonBlue chooses them,
gives the team an honest plan with no guarantee in it, and gets them out. The
festival ends half-lit and true. Only then, behind him, does one cyan segment of
a much larger symbol come alive.

## The emotional question

> Can NeonBlue acknowledge real danger without feeling that he has betrayed hope?

The issue answers: yes, and the hope is better for it.

The distinction that matters, and that every NeonBlue line should be tested
against: **reassurance is about the speaker; honesty is about the listener.**
NeonBlue's promises in the first half are things he needs to say. His plan in
the second half is a thing the team needs to hear. Same character, same warmth,
different function.

He does not become grim. He stops requiring the outcome to be guaranteed before
he will move toward it.

## Why the Echo fires late

The season bible is explicit that the relay activates **only after** the choice.
This is the load-bearing rule of the issue, and the legacy version broke it.

If the system reacts to NeonBlue's presence, the story says he was already
special and the plot merely confirms it. If it reacts to his choice, the story
says the choice made him legible to something that had been listening for
exactly that. The second is the season's thesis: *the Echo did not create these
qualities, it recognised them.*

Practically: page 4 gets an ambiguous flicker that Lil Devil's interference
could explain. Page 8 gets the real thing. The reader should be able to
reinterpret page 4 after page 8, which is a better experience than being told
twice.

Recorded as conflict **C-01**. Owner confirmed 2026-08-13: follow the season bible.

## Why Lil Devil causes the discovery

The guest's job in this season is to apply pressure the featured character must
absorb, and to learn one thing. Lil Devil's growth seed is *restraint can direct
power*.

That lesson needs two halves. In the first, he uses force without direction and
something unintended happens — he forces the service gate and the system answers
in a way nobody expected. In the second, he uses force with direction and it
works. Without the first half he is a tool, not a character, and the discovery
has no author.

Recorded as conflict **C-02**. Owner confirmed 2026-08-13: restore the guest function.

## Cast functions

Every character has one job. Nobody gets a line that another character could
deliver.

| Character | Job | Beats |
|---|---|---|
| **NeonBlue** | The decision, the plan, the closing internal beat | Throughout |
| **Lil Devil** | Uncontrolled force that reveals; directed force that works | P2, P4, P7 |
| **Moodz** | Names that NeonBlue is managing his own fear, without demanding he stop | P5 |
| **TwoTone** | Reads two converging paths; supplies the route at the climax | P3, P7 |
| **Static** | Feels it first; hears the six-part pulse; calls the timing window | P2, P3, P7 |
| **Ash** | One precise line at the point of denial | P4 |
| **Scarline** | Warns that the crowd's panic is not the danger; confirms the choice is real without making it | P3, P6 |

Ash speaks once. Scarline speaks twice. That is not neglect — it is their
register. The season bible is explicit that Ash should never be made talkative
to carry exposition, and that Scarline delivers one clear sentence, once.

## What this issue must not do

- Resolve Patch. The marker is a wound, not an answer.
- Light more than one of six Echo segments.
- Date or explain FusionZoo infrastructure.
- Let a guest solve the central problem.
- Make NeonBlue foolish because he is hopeful.
- Make Static's alarm a false alarm — the disturbance is real.
- Give Scarline a facial scar or make her cold.
- Put glasses on anyone.
- Injure the trapped group. The danger is being overlooked, not being harmed.
