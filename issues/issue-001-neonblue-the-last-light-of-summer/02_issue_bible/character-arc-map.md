# Issue 001 — Character Arc Map

Who changes, who doesn't, and where each beat lands.

Panels are addressed `P<page>-<panel>`.

---

## NeonBlue (MZ-CHAR-005) — featured

**Arc** Treats reassurance as part of rescue → discovers reassurance is
something he needs → gives an honest plan instead → can call an imperfect night
good.

| Page | Beat | What the reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| P1-01 | Volunteers the group before anyone agrees | Energy, generosity, no consultation |
| P1-03 | Guarantees the backup will hold | Certainty offered as a gift |
| P2-02 | The backup fails while he is still speaking | The gap between promise and fact |
| P3-03 | Reassurance gets brighter and faster | Effort becoming visible |
| P4-02 | Denies the pattern out loud | The last defence |
| P4-03 | Ash: "Hope can read warnings." | Named from outside |
| P5-01 | Finds Patch's frequency on the marker | The fear becomes specific |
| P5-02 | Moodz names it kindly | Permission to stop performing |
| P6-02 | Sees the trapped group and the countdown at once | The choice arrives |
| P6-03 | Chooses the corridor | The decision, silent |
| P7-01 | "No guarantee. Here's how we get them out." | Honesty as leadership |
| P8-01 | "Everyone walked out. That's a good night." | The new baseline |
| P8-03 | Asks why the relay knew him | The season opens |

**Register note.** His voice does not get darker. It gets *specific*. Early
lines are about everyone and nothing ("completely normal festival feature").
Late lines are about these people and this hinge. Same warmth, aimed.

**Trait budget** One defining trait (refuses to abandon people), one minor
behaviour (over-volunteering, used twice then dropped). No catchphrase.

## Lil Devil (MZ-CHAR-LILDEVIL) — guest

**Arc** Force as the first answer → force produces something nobody wanted →
aimed force works.

| Page | Beat |
|---|---|
| P1-02 | Offers to bypass the queue by force. Comic, harmless |
| P2-03 | Forces the service gate against advice |
| P4-01 | The system reacts to the interference — the discovery, and he caused it |
| P7-03 | Strikes exactly where NeonBlue marks. "Precision destruction." |

**Restrictions** He does not cause the core problem, does not choose, does not
complete the rescue, and does not learn out loud. The lesson is visible in the
last beat, not stated.

## Moodz (MZ-CHAR-001) — meaningful secondary

One beat, load-bearing.

| Page | Beat |
|---|---|
| P3-03 | Watches NeonBlue perform. Says nothing |
| P5-02 | "Your smile's wider than usual. You don't have to sell safety to us." |

Moodz protects choice. He does not tell NeonBlue to stop; he removes the
obligation. The distinction is his whole character.

## Ash (MZ-CHAR-004) — functional beat

One line in the issue.

| Page | Beat |
|---|---|
| P4-03 | "Hope can read warnings." |

Placed at the exact point NeonBlue is denying the risk, and it changes what
happens next. The season bible warns against making Ash talkative to carry
exposition; he carries none.

## Scarline (MZ-CHAR-006) — observational

| Page | Beat |
|---|---|
| P3-01 | "Dark isn't the problem. What people do in it is." |
| P6-03 | Confirms the choice in front of NeonBlue is real, without making it |

Her second beat is the one the legacy version lacked. She validates; she does
not rescue. She must not say "go save them" — that would take the choice away,
which is precisely what her character exists not to do.

## What must not happen

- NeonBlue becoming foolish, or naive, or sad.
- Static being wrong, panicked for comedy, or told to calm down.
- Moodz absorbing NeonBlue's feelings rather than protecting his choice.
- Scarline making the decision for NeonBlue.
- Ash speaking a second time.
- Lil Devil completing the rescue.
- Any character announcing their own personality.
