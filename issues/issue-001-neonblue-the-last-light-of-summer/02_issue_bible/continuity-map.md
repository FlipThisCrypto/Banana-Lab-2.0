# Issue 001 — Continuity Map

What this issue inherits, what it establishes, and what it must leave intact
for the five issues that follow.

---

## Inherited

Nothing. This is the season opener and must be readable with no prior knowledge.

Everything a reader needs — who the six are, what the festival is, that
something is wrong with the old infrastructure — is established on the page.

The one exception is **Patch**, who is referred to but never explained. That is
deliberate: the reader should feel the weight of a name NeonBlue does not want
to say, without being handed a backstory. Patch has a character bible
(`MZ-CHAR-PATCH`) but no approved visual and does not appear.

## Required for later issues

### Issue 002 — Static, "Signals in the Silence"

- Static must hear a **six-part pulse** here, not a generic noise. Issue 002
  turns on Clever isolating six distinct tones, one of which matches this
  issue's cyan frequency.
- Static's difficulty is **translation**, not perception. He must be right and
  hard to follow, never wrong or hysterical.
- The cyan frequency must be specific enough to be recognised again.

### Issue 003 — Scarline, "The House That Remembers"

- The old infrastructure must feel *old* without being dated. No age is stated.
- Scarline must not reveal that she has prior experience with the system. Her
  page-6 beat is validation, not recognition.

### Issue 006 — TwoTone, "Two Sides of Tomorrow"

- Exactly one segment lights here. Five remain.
- The relay must not command, threaten or speak. It reacts. Establishing it as
  an entity with intent would break the finale's argument.
- The possibility that the network could locate Patch must remain live — it is
  half the cost of the finale's Path Two.

## Visual continuity locks

| Element | Lock |
|---|---|
| Wardrobe | Default for all seven characters. No changes, no damage, no additions. One continuous evening. |
| Time | Single evening, dusk to night. No scene occurs the next day. |
| Weather | Clear, warm, dry. No rain. Ground is dry — this matters for reflections. |
| Echo symbol | Only one segment ever visible. Never the whole symbol, never two. |
| Cyan | Reserved. Cyan light means the system, except where it is NeonBlue's own colour. Do not use cyan decoratively in the festival palette. |
| Relay marker | Same object, same markings, every appearance. |
| Nonnegotiable canon | Season bible §21 applies in full. No glasses on anyone. Scarline has no facial scar. |

## Continuity risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Cyan used decoratively in festival lighting, diluting its meaning | Festival palette is amber, magenta and warm white. Cyan appears only where the system does. |
| The Echo segment reading as a logo rather than a fragment | It must be partial, off-centre, and clearly incomplete. |
| NeonBlue reading as naive rather than effortful | Every reassurance is placed where the reader can see the fear underneath. |
| Patch becoming an explanation | Patch is named once, by NeonBlue, and never discussed. |
| The trapped group becoming victims | They are inconvenienced and frightened, not harmed. Nobody is injured. |
| Extras drifting off-model | Extras are silhouettes and simplified figures, never near-cast lookalikes. |

## Post-issue continuity questions

The season bible requires these ten to be answered after the script is complete.
Answered against the rebuilt script:

1. **Did the featured character make the decisive choice?** Yes — P6-03, P7-01.
2. **Did the guest remain supporting?** Yes — Lil Devil causes a discovery and
   applies directed force; he does not choose or complete the rescue.
3. **Did every supporting character serve a purpose?** Yes — all five have named
   beats.
4. **Was the origin revealed through action?** Yes — the relay marker is an
   object encountered, not a flashback.
5. **Was only one Echo layer added?** Yes — one segment, on page 8.
6. **Did the issue answer one question and create another?** Yes — answers
   "can hope tell the truth and still lead?"; asks "why did the relay recognise
   NeonBlue?"
7. **Did the story preserve unresolved naming and visual canon?** Yes.
8. **Did any character behave outside their bible?** No.
9. **Did the issue overuse quirks, phrases or exposition?** No — one running
   gag, used twice and dropped; no catchphrases.
10. **What new facts require owner approval?** The six listed under *Proposed
    story canon* above, plus conflicts C-01 and C-02.
